Financial Review Report

Sales Invoice & Purchase Invoice — Accounting Correctness
Project: Operix (Flutter Desktop, PostgreSQL)  |  Date: 2026-06-16  |  Reviewed by: Senior Finance Consultant (AI)

Executive Summary

The double-entry mechanics are sound — every sales and purchase invoice posts a balanced journal entry, account resolution fails loudly, and headers/lines/stock/GL are wrapped in one transaction. However, there is one structural defect that silently corrupts both the Balance Sheet and the P&L on every sale once purchase prices vary: inventory is capitalized at actual cost but relieved at "latest cost." Several supporting gaps compound the risk.

Detailed Findings

❌ Wrong  THE BIG ISSUE — Inventory capitalized at actual cost, relieved at "latest cost"

Purchase invoices debit Inventory at the actual purchase cost (postLine('1300','debit', subtotal), postgres_purchase_invoice_repository.dart:160) and then overwrite the product's cost with the latest purchase price (cost_price = @unit_price, postgres_purchase_invoice_repository.dart:79) — there is no weighted-average or FIFO calculation anywhere in the codebase (verified by full-repo grep).

Sales invoices then relieve Inventory / charge COGS at quantity × current cost_price (cogs = Σ costPrice × qty, sales_invoice_models.dart:49; posted at postgres_sales_invoice_repository.dart:166).

Because the cost that goes in (actual) differs from the cost that comes out (latest), the Inventory control account (1300) drifts from physical reality, COGS is misstated every period, and Inventory can go negative even when stock is physically positive. Latest-cost valuation is not permitted under IAS 2 (only FIFO or weighted average).

Worked proof (EGP)

StepActionDr InventoryCr Inventory (COGS)Inventory GL bal.
1Buy 10 @ 10100.00—100.00
2Buy 10 @ 20 (cost_price → 20)200.00—300.00
3Sell all 20 @ cost 20 each—400.00−100.00

Cost in = 300; cost out = 400. COGS overstated by 100, gross profit understated by 100, Inventory ends at −100 instead of 0. The Inventory account can never tie to a physical count.

Recommendation — Critical

Adopt moving weighted average consistently. On purchase, replace the overwrite with: new_cost = (qoh*cost_price + qty*unit_price) / (qoh + qty), and relieve COGS at that same cost_price at sale time. Capitalize the same basis you relieve. (FIFO is the alternative but needs explicit cost layers.) Add a periodic reconciliation report: Inventory GL (1300) vs. Σ(qoh × cost_price).

🔍 Not Implemented  POS sales post nothing to the General Ledger

postgres_pos_repository.dart checkout relieves stock and records movements but writes zero journal entries — no Revenue (4100), no Tax Payable (2200), no COGS (5100)/Inventory (1300). For a POS-first product, the primary sales channel is invisible to the ledger; revenue, output VAT and COGS are absent from the books. Same posting gap as the sales-invoice path, arguably higher impact by volume.

Recommendation — Critical

Post the same balanced revenue + COGS journal on POS checkout (cash/card debit instead of A/R). Reuse the sales-invoice posting routine. Tie the day's POS revenue to the shift reconciliation and the cash GL account.

⚠️ Risk  Invoice numbering can have gaps (legal/compliance)

Numbers come from nextval('sales_invoice_seq') (postgres_sales_invoice_repository.dart:31) inside the transaction, but the stock guard throws and rolls back after the number is already consumed (:85–89). PostgreSQL sequences are non-transactional, so every failed posting burns a number → permanent gaps. Egypt ETA and Saudi ZATCA both mandate gapless sequential invoice numbering.

Recommendation — High

Validate stock and all preconditions before drawing the invoice number, or allocate the number from a gap-tolerant ledger table updated within the transaction. Reconcile issued numbers for gaps as a control report.

🔍 Not Implemented  Sales / purchase returns have no GL reversal

The sales_returns table exists (migration 006) and returns restock physically, but no repository posts a reversing journal (Dr Revenue + Dr Tax / Cr A/R or Cash, and Cr COGS / Dr Inventory). Revenue, output VAT and COGS remain overstated after a refund.

Recommendation — High

Post a credit-note journal on every return that references the original invoice number, reversing revenue, tax, and COGS at the original cost basis.

⚠️ Risk  No database-level balance enforcement

The GL schema (004_gl_schema.sql) has no trigger asserting Σdebit = Σcredit per journal_entry. Balance depends entirely on app arithmetic, and postLine silently skips any non-positive amount (if (!amount.isPositive) return;) — a single bad input could post a one-sided entry that the DB would accept.

Recommendation — Medium

Add a deferred constraint trigger that rejects any journal entry whose debit and credit totals differ. This is the cheap insurance every accounting core should carry.

⚠️ Risk  Tax model is rigid and mixes input/output VAT

Tax rate is hardcoded to 14% (sales_invoice_models.dart:31), exclusive-only, with a single 2200 Tax Payable account carrying both output VAT (sales credit) and input VAT (purchase debit). There is no zero-rated vs. exempt distinction, no line-level tax, and no discount field on the invoice at all. Workable for a single-rate Egypt MVP; blocks UAE/Saudi multi-rate and mixed-supply invoices.

Recommendation — Medium

Separate Output VAT (2200, liability) from Input VAT (e.g. 1400, recoverable asset); drive the rate from a tax table; support zero-rated/exempt flags and invoice discounts. Add a recoverable/non-recoverable flag on purchase input VAT.

✅ Correct  Double-entry balance and transactional integrity

Both invoice types post mathematically balanced entries (Sales: Dr A/R total = Cr Revenue subtotal + Cr Tax + the COGS pair nets to zero; Purchase: Dr Inventory + Dr Tax = Cr A/P total). Account codes are resolved up front and fail loudly if misconfigured, and header + lines + stock + GL share one runTx. The Money value object uses 6-decimal intermediate precision rounding to 2-decimal storage. Mechanics are good — the defect is the cost basis, not the bookkeeping.
